A therapeutic massage isn't meant to put you to sleep. It's meant to work out a pain that has settled in, whether it comes from sitting at a desk every day, one wrong movement, or tension that has been building in the shoulders for months.

What people come to us for

  • Lower back pain. The first area to give in to office chairs and bad lifting.
  • Neck pain and a stiff neck. The feeling that you can't turn your head fully to one side.
  • Locked shoulders. Raised towards the ears from stress or hours at a laptop.
  • Knots that keep coming back. The same two or three points that tighten up again.
  • Reduced mobility. The body feels stiff, especially in the morning.

How the session goes

  1. We talk for two or three minutes: where it hurts, for how long, what you've already tried.
  2. We warm the area with hot stones and natural oil, so the muscle gives way more easily.
  3. We work precisely on knots and trigger points, with firm, controlled pressure.
  4. We finish with light stretches and slow strokes, so the area stays free rather than irritated.

How much it hurts

Honestly: on an old knot, the first minutes can be uncomfortable. It's the kind of discomfort that feels like it's working, not the kind that scares you. Our rule is simple. If you tense up or hold your breath, it's too much: say so and we ease off straight away.

How many sessions you need

For a recent problem, one session usually brings relief you feel the same evening. For pain that has been there for months or years, one session won't erase it. In those cases we recommend 3 or 4 sessions a week apart, then one a month for maintenance. After the first session we tell you honestly what we think can be achieved.

Massage does not treat a slipped disc or any spinal condition. If the pain runs down a leg or arm, or comes with numbness or weakness, see a doctor first.

What is the difference between therapeutic and relaxation massage?

Relaxation massage is slow and gentle and aims to calm you down. Therapeutic massage starts from a specific pain and works on it precisely, with firm pressure.

Will I be sore the next day?

Sometimes, mildly, on the areas that were worked hardest. It passes quickly.
